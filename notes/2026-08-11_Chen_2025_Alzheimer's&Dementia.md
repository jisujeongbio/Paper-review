## Why we selected this paper
- This study presented an interesting perspective on Alzheimer's disease(AD) research by focusing on **resilience to AD pathology** rather than finding risk factors such as APOE and TREM2.
- Since my previous research experience has primarily involved transcriptomic level analyses, including RNA-seq and spatial transcirptomics analysis, this paper provided a valuable opportunity to learn about and understand the **proteomics analysis workflow**.
- This paper used diverse statistical and bioinformatics approaches to derive biological insights from complex omic data, closely aligning with the research direction I hope to pursue.
 
## Main findings
1. Genetic background strongly influenced cognitive resilience in AD-BXD mice despite the same 5xFAD transgene.
2. Proteomics and pQTL analysis identified nine resilience-associated proteins, eight of which mapped to a shared chromosome 1 locus.
3. Mediation analysis suggested that the Chr1 haplotype affects cognitive resilience indirectly through coordinated protein abundance changes.
4. Nr1d1 was proposed as a key regulator linking the genetic variant to metabolic and mitochondrial protein networks.

## Discussion notes
- This study focused on resilience rather than AD risk, providing a different perspective on disease heterogeneity.
- The Nr1d1 effect appeared to be sex- and age-specific, mainly in 6-month-old female mice.
- (Jewoo) CRISPR-based perturbation of the eight resilience-associated proteins could help test whether they causally affect cognitive resilience.

## Personal notes
- I expect that a similar strategy could be applied to human cohort datasets, like the UK biobank, to study resilience by integrating genetic variants with pQTLs and other molecular phenotypes.
- I was impressed by how the study moved beyond describing protein-level changes and tried to identify the upstream regulatory mechanisms behind them. This approach reminded me of the importance of looking beyond the observed phenotype and asking what upstream mechanisms may regulate it.
